### Post 6 - COR vs CORE (Long Educational Post)

**What Are You Looking At? COR or CORE?**

The left pic shows the result of farrier forced COR principles on the front hoof of this 22 yr old Grand Prix level Warmblood mare. The red area is of the most concern to me, as this is where arthritic changes appear. In this pic, there are signs of strain, atrophy, and collapse. The green area is the living supple hoof capsule that can be used to assess progress as it grows down. The blue area is the retained dead hoof capsule that should grow all the way out for a full rehab. This "old foot/new foot" stage occurs when the hoof capsule is allowed to grow more than it's allowed to wear, or be properly trimmed to simulate wear.

The right pic shows the same hoof 18 months later, after corrective and rehabilitative trimming to restore this horse's true angles. You can see that the hoof is fully live, the retained dead hoof capsule has been grown off, and growth/wear equilibrium has been restored. There is no blue area. The red area has been "pumped up", showing evidence of significant healing.

I believe in the importance of the COR principles. I just don't believe in propping the horse up on false heel height, steel shoes, wedges and DIM to get there prematurely. If there was an accompanying x-ray with the pic on the left, you would see that although there is a slightly straighter HPA, the spacing of the lower 3 joints may not be aligned, and the navicular bone may also be out of alignment and jammed up into the coffin joint. This is where the "crumpled" effect in the foot on the left comes from. There is not enough soft tissue and live sole depth for a proper elastic foundation.

My focus goes less to the angles and more to the health and strength of the bare hoof. I believe that should be optimized before considering a prosthetic, unless the prosthetic is actually going to improve the health and strength of the foot, rather than just keep the horse going for a couple more years.

The problem is that most people are looking at X-rays that only show things like boney alignment and sole depth. People are hyper focused on HPA and PA instead of soft tissue, live sole depth, and joint spacing. There is plenty of, if not more, valuable information on the outside of the hoof. You just need to read the obvious writing on the wall and trust your gut more.

Frequent proper trimming to simulate natural wear and promote healthy development works by knowing the shapes of the internal anatomy and finding the natural shapes in the external anatomy to achieve the true hoof capsule/P3 alignment. There are more curves than straight lines, flat planes, and angles inside and out. These curves need to be respected and built over time to achieve optimal alignment and integrity.

The shaping of the outside of the foot influences the development of the inside of the foot, which again goes back to influencing the outside of the foot.

Imo, The COR principles should only be used to assess progress, not as a forced ideal. True strength comes from building the "core" of the digital cushion (collecting the foot), not from flattening and propping a weak foot up on a false external foundation.

*Maronda has an album on Hoof Builders. She has been in training for the entire rehab process and has shown at least 3 times during these 18 months. A full rehab typically takes me 24 months. Fully live well developed feet typically take me 4 years. I can see visible improvement in the feet for up to 6 years. I can see improvements in movement beyond that. Age and time in shoes or improperly trimmed feet are also factors, so I always recommend starting to build the feet as early as possible and if you're going to use shoes, avoid flat trimming, metal, and wedges.*
